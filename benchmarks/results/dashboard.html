TokenMizer vs. other memory methods — 100-session test

We tested 8 memory methods on the same 100 test conversations, using the same scoring rules for all of them. TokenMizer is the real product (version 0.5.3). The other 7 are rebuilt versions of the strategy used by MemGPT, Mem0, Graphiti/Zep, and GraphRAG — they do not use an AI model, so treat them as a lower bound on what those real products could do. Full write-up: benchmarks/results/REPORT_n100.md.

The main finding: clear language vs. indirect language

Every generated conversation is labeled by how directly it states facts — from clear markers like "Decided: X" down to facts buried inside a longer sentence with no marker word. All 4 pattern-matching methods, including TokenMizer, drop to around 19–25% accuracy once the marker words disappear — a drop of more than 45 points from their best score. This happens to every method that relies on pattern matching, not just TokenMizer. (Unspecified = the 6 real conversations, which were not labeled this way.)

TokenMizer compared to each method, one by one

How much better (or worse) TokenMizer scored than each other method, on the same conversations. The line through each bar shows the range where the true difference likely falls. If that range crosses zero, we can't be sure there's a real difference.

In plain terms: TokenMizer beats GraphRAG-style (+15.6 points), MemGPT-style (+24.6 points), and every simple baseline (+39 to +43 points) — these are real, reliable wins. Against Graphiti-style and Mem0-style, the range still crosses zero, meaning it's a tie, not a win — but after we found and fixed two extraction bugs (see the report's Section 9), TokenMizer's score moved from slightly behind both to slightly ahead of both, while staying a statistical tie either way. TokenMizer's output is also smaller (100 tokens vs. 117–119 tokens).
